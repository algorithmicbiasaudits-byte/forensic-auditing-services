The Compliance Gap

Your ATS Made a Decision.
Did Anyone Review It?

Companies using AI to screen candidates face a critical compliance gap. AI systems reject candidates in seconds — but compliance reviews happen weeks or months later, if at all. By then, the liability is done.

NYC LL144, Illinois HB 3773 (active since January 2026), Colorado SB 189 (effective January 1, 2027), and the EU AI Act already impose real compliance obligations on AI-driven hiring decisions — and Title VII and EEOC UGESP liability never went away, even though federal AI-specific guidance was withdrawn in 2025. Your organization needs to audit before rejections execute — not after they become complaints.

Jurisdiction Coverage

Every Active Framework. One System.

F.A.S. tracks all enforced and upcoming AI hiring laws so your compliance posture is always current.

⚖️
EEOC UGESP — The Federal Baseline
29 C.F.R. Part 1607 · In force since 1978
● Active — All Employers
  • 4/5ths rule: selection rate < 80% of highest group = adverse impact
  • Defense: job-related + consistent with business necessity
  • 2-year applicant flow data retention required
  • AI tools confirmed as "selection procedures" (EEOC 2023)
⚠ EEOC guidance was removed from EEOC.gov in January 2025 — but 29 C.F.R. Part 1607 remains federal law and is fully enforceable.
🗽
NYC Local Law 144
NYC Admin. Code §20-870 · Effective July 5, 2023
● Active
  • Annual bias audit by independent third party required
  • 10-day advance notice to candidates before AEDT use
  • Audit results published on company website
  • $500–$1,500 per violation per day
🏔️
Colorado SB 189 (ADMT Act)
C.R.S. §6-1-1801 et seq. · Signed May 14, 2026
⚠ Effective January 1, 2027
  • Pre-use notice to consumers required
  • Post-adverse-outcome disclosure within 30 days
  • 3-year record retention
  • 60-day cure period before penalty
Annual impact assessments, risk management programs, and duty-of-care provisions — all REMOVED from prior bill. Notice-only framework.
May 2024SB 205 passed — comprehensive requirements including annual audits
Aug 2025SB 205 delayed to June 30, 2026
May 12, 2026SB 189 passed — full repeal and replacement of SB 205
May 14, 2026Governor Polis signed SB 189 into law
Jun 30, 2026SB 205 NEVER takes effect — superseded by SB 189
Jan 1, 2027SB 189 (current law) takes effect
🌾
Illinois HB 3773
Pub. Act 103-0795 · Effective January 1, 2026
● Active
  • AI notice to employees and applicants required
  • Strict liability for discriminatory effects — intent irrelevant
  • Zip code proxy discrimination explicitly banned
  • 4-year record retention
🏛️
OFCCP — Federal Contractors
41 C.F.R. Parts 60-1, 60-300, 60-741
● Active — Contractors Only
  • Applies: $50K+ contracts (Section 503) / $150K+ (VEVRAA)
  • Written AAP required at $50K+ with 50+ employees
  • VEVRAA veteran hiring benchmark: 5.1%
  • 2-year retention: AI inputs, logs, hiring records
  • Vendor contracts must include audit access clause
Federal contractors and subcontractors only. Activate in F.A.S. settings if your organization holds qualifying federal contracts.
☀️
California — CRC FEHA + CPPA ADMT
2 CCR §11008 et seq. (FEHA) · Cal. Code Regs. tit. 11 (CPPA) · FEHA in force Oct 1, 2025
● Active — FEHA in force; CPPA pending 2027
  • FEHA ADS regulations: disparate impact prohibition on any employment ADS, 5+ employee threshold
  • FEHA: absence of anti-bias testing is evidence of liability; independent audits strengthen the affirmative defense
  • FEHA: 4-year retention for all ADS inputs, outputs, and decision criteria
  • CPPA ADMT (eff. Jan 1, 2027): pre-use notice, risk assessments, opt-out rights for "significant decisions"
AB 1018 and the earlier AB 2930 both stalled/were ordered inactive — neither is law. California's real framework is the FEHA/CPPA regulatory track above, not a named AI bill.
🇪🇺
EU AI Act
Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 · High-risk employment AI
◑ Effective August 2, 2026
  • Employment AI classified as high-risk
  • Human oversight of high-risk decisions required
  • Conformity assessment before deployment
  • Up to €35M or 7% of global annual turnover

How F.A.S. Works

Five steps between an ATS rejection and a legally defensible outcome.

1

ATS Flags Rejection

F.A.S.'s deterministic audit engine analyzes the decision for proxy discrimination, 4/5ths rule violations, keyword mismatch, and employment gap bias — rule-based math, not LLM guesswork.
